# Brand naming: Vesconte

## Decision

The product is named **Vesconte**, after **Pietro Vesconte** (fl. 1310–1330), the
Genoese cartographer who worked in Venice and who is generally credited as the
first professional mapmaker to sign and date his charts as a matter of habit.

`Longbrunch` and `Spy Signal` are retired. They are not aliases, not fallbacks,
and not acceptable in new identifiers, copy, metadata, storage keys, or
documentation.

## Why this name

Four things about Vesconte's work map onto what this product is trying to be.

### 1. He signed and dated the evidence

Vesconte's 1311 portolan chart of the eastern Mediterranean is the oldest
surviving signed and dated nautical chart. Before him, charts circulated
anonymously and undated, so a navigator had no way to tell how old a coastline
was or who was accountable for it.

This is the product's central discipline. Every number carries a source, an
as-of timestamp, and a lineage. Coverage gaps are marked as unavailable rather
than filled with an estimate. A figure nobody will sign is a figure the product
does not print.

### 2. He drew the relationships, not the destination

A portolan chart is not a route. It is a network of rhumb lines radiating from
compass roses across the whole basin, letting a navigator read the bearing
between any two points and decide for themselves. Vesconte's 32-line rhumb
network, colour-coded to separate the principal winds from the half and quarter
winds, is a legibility system, not an instruction.

The product works the same way. It shows relationships between assets, market
layers, and company evidence, and it deliberately stops short of turning them
into a trade instruction. The reader steers.

### 3. He assembled layers into one map

Vesconte's 1318 atlas is ten sheets that can be laid out together to form a
single large map. Each sheet stands alone; together they are a wider picture. He
also drew the mappa mundi bound into Marino Sanudo's *Liber secretorum fidelium
crucis*, sitting a world view beside a working chart.

The product keeps market, company, and fund evidence in separate layers that
each hold up on their own, and lets the reader compose them into one view.

### 4. He improved accuracy incrementally, in public

Vesconte did not invent the portolan chart. He inherited a thirteenth-century
form and made it measurably better, particularly the coastlines of northern
Europe and Britain, across successive dated editions. The dating is what makes
the improvement checkable.

That is the intended cadence here: versioned methodology, visible revisions, and
claims that can be checked against the edition that made them.

## Pronunciation and usage

- Written **Vesconte**. Capital V, no accent, no space.
- Lowercase `vesconte` is acceptable as a wordmark and in domains and handles.
- **Pietro Vesconte** is used only when telling the origin story. The product is
  never "Pietro".
- Never "Vesconte AI", "Vesconte Research", or any suffixed variant, unless a
  separate approved decision creates one.
